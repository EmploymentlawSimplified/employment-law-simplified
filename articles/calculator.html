How the figures are worked out

​

1. Basic award

Calculated like statutory redundancy pay. For each complete year of service (up to 20 years) you get:

  • 1.5 weeks' pay for each full year you were aged 41 or over
  • 1 week's pay for each full year you were aged 22 to 40
  • 0.5 week's pay for each full year you were under 22

A week's pay is capped at £719 (from 6 April 2025), so the maximum basic award is £21,570.

​

2. Compensatory award

This covers your actual financial loss — mainly lost earnings from the date of dismissal until you find comparable work (past and estimated future). It is capped at the lower of 52 weeks' gross pay or £118,223 (from 6 April 2025).

​
Duty to mitigate

A tribunal expects you to take reasonable steps to find new work. Keep evidence of every job you apply for — it protects your compensatory award.

​

3. Injury to feelings (discrimination claims)

If your claim involves discrimination, you may also recover compensation for injury to feelings, awarded in the three “Vento” bands (for claims presented on or after 6 April 2025):

  • Lower band — £1,200 to £12,100: one-off or isolated incidents
  • Middle band — £12,100 to £36,400: serious cases that don't merit the top band
  • Upper band — £36,400 to £60,700: the most serious, e.g. a lengthy campaign of harassment

Discrimination compensation is not capped, which is why discrimination claims can be worth considerably more than “ordinary” unfair dismissal.

​
This is a starting point, not a verdict

Real awards are affected by ACAS uplifts (up to 25%), contributory fault deductions, Polkey reductions, pension loss, grossing-up for tax, and more. Treat this number as a way to understand the shape of a claim — then get your facts and evidence in order.
